# Official sites supplement; real photos decide accuracy

No ready-made Malaysian make-and-model dataset exists, so the training set has to be assembled from four tiers: openly licensed photos (Wikimedia Commons, Flickr CC), a filtered copy of the 97,598-image Hugging Face crawl of Malaysian-market vehicles, self-photographed cars, and official manufacturer images as a training-only supplement. Official websites are a weak primary source: they show only the current facelift of models on sale, the images that could be inspected are clean CGI renders, and the yield is roughly 1,000 to 1,500 images across Perodua, Proton and MINI, about 1,150 of them near-duplicate Proton 360-degree spin frames. The large academic sets (Stanford Cars, CompCars, VMMRdb, DVM-CAR) are useful for pretraining only, since none could be confirmed to contain Perodua or Proton. Classifieds such as Mudah.my hold the biggest pool of labelled Malaysian photos (4,161 live Myvi listings) but Mudah's terms forbid scraping and downloading without written consent, and most rivals block automated requests. Benchmark evidence says about 40 clean images per class is enough for 90%+ accuracy with a strong pretrained backbone, so quantity is not the binding constraint; coverage of older generations, real-world conditions and a leak-free split are. The single most important design rule is to split by vehicle or listing rather than by image, and to test only on real photos: on CompCars, removing near-duplicates from the test side cut measured accuracy from about 90% to about 61%. Several figures below are estimates or could not be verified, and they are flagged where they appear.

## Official websites give about 1,000 to 1,500 renders of current models only

The official sites answer one question well (what does the current model look like?) and almost nothing else. Perodua's navigation lists eight models: Traz, QV-E, Axia, Bezza, Myvi, Ativa, Alza and Aruz ([perodua.com.my](https://www.perodua.com.my/)). Proton's main site carries model pages for just five nameplates (X50, X70, X90, S70, Saga), with no Persona, Iriz or e.MAS pages in its sitemap ([proton.com/sitemap.xml](https://www.proton.com/sitemap.xml)), even though a third-party price list still shows ten Proton models on sale, including Persona, Iriz and the e.MAS range ([zigwheels.my](https://www.zigwheels.my/new-cars/proton)). MINI Malaysia covers the new-generation Cooper 3-Door, 5-Door, Convertible and Electric, plus Aceman, Countryman and JCW ([mini.my](https://www.mini.my/en_MY/home.html)). Honda Malaysia lists nine models ([honda.com.my](https://www.honda.com.my/)). Toyota Malaysia's line-up could not be read because the site renders client-side, and the BYD Malaysia and Proton e.MAS sites were unreachable during research.

**Proton is the richest and easiest to harvest.** Each model's colours page embeds a 360-degree exterior spin of 48 PNG frames per colour, giving 240 frames each for the Saga, X50, X90 and S70 (five colours) and 192 for the X70 (four colours), counted directly from the page data ([Saga colours](https://www.proton.com/models/all-new-saga/colours), [X70 colours](https://www.proton.com/models/x70/colours)). The frame URLs sit under random eight-character folder IDs, so they must be read from the page source rather than generated. A downloaded sample frame was a 1200x375 RGBA PNG with about 41% transparent pixels: a clean CGI front three-quarter render with no number plate, surroundings or occupants ([sample frame](https://cms-assets.proton.com/proton-cms-blob/media/ptrnmsxk/saga-mc3-premium-2025-marine-blue_0001.png)). Only one trim per model is spun.

Perodua and MINI expose far less in static HTML. The Myvi page offers a key visual, five colour thumbnails, four variant thumbnails and a few feature images, and the page itself states that product images are "for illustration purpose only" ([Perodua Myvi](https://www.perodua.com.my/our-models/myvi.html)). MINI's Cooper 3-Door page has four exterior gallery images (overview, front, side, rear) drawn from shared global assets rather than Malaysian photography ([mini.my Cooper 3-Door](https://www.mini.my/en_MY/home/range/mini-cooper-3-door.html)). The researchers estimate 5 to 15 usable exterior images per Perodua model and 4 to 8 per MINI body style; these are estimates, not counts. Whether Perodua has a true multi-frame 360 viewer, and how many views the MINI configurator serves, was not verified and needs a manual browser check.

Three limits make these images a supplement rather than a foundation. First, every site shows only current models and current facelifts: Proton's spin assets are named for the Saga MC3 2025 and the 2026 X70, X90 and S70 updates ([proton.com/sitemap.xml](https://www.proton.com/sitemap.xml)), so the 2019 to 2025 Saga, older Myvi generations, Viva, Kancil, Wira, Persona and Iriz, which dominate the roads, are absent. Second, the 240 Proton frames per model are one 3D model seen from 48 angles in five paints, with no variation in lighting, background, trim, wheels or camera height. Third, the domain gap is real: in a study of five product classes, render-only training with 2,000 renders per class scored 76.0% against 83.3% for real-only training with 96 real images per class, and adding just 12 to 24 real images per class recovered most of the difference ([arXiv 2212.04790](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.04790)). No study was found that measures manufacturer renders against street photos for cars specifically, so the size of the gap for this project is unknown until it is measured on a real-photo test set.

The transparent background is the one genuine advantage. Proton frames can be pasted onto street and car-park backgrounds with scale, blur and lighting augmentation. Official images also serve as a correctly labelled visual reference when auditing noisier sources.

On terms, every site reserves copyright and none mentions scraping or machine learning. Perodua limits the site to private, non-commercial use and exempts such use from its written-consent requirement for reproduction ([Perodua Terms](https://www.perodua.com.my/legal-notices/terms-of-use.html)); Mazda Malaysia similarly authorises viewing and downloading for personal, non-commercial use ([Mazda Terms](https://mazda.com.my/terms-conditions/)). Proton and Honda demand express prior written consent for any copying, with no personal-use carve-out ([Proton Terms](https://www.proton.com/terms-and-conditions), [Honda Terms](https://www.honda.com.my/terms)). MINI bars copying for commercial use or distribution and reposting ([mini.my Legal](https://www.mini.my/en_MY/home/footer/legal.html)). Nissan is strictest, forbidding storage in any format, and its robots.txt disallows the image upload folder ([Nissan disclaimer](https://nissan.com.my/disclaimer/), [robots.txt](https://www.nissan.com.my/robots.txt)). The practical reading, which is not legal advice: Perodua and Mazda plausibly cover a private student download; for Proton and Honda the clean route is an email asking permission; and no site permits republishing the images, so share code and URL lists, never the files. The only official source with real location photography and older generations is the BMW Group press portal, which hosts MINI photo sets back to 2001 but licenses them for "press purposes exclusively" ([BMW Newsroom legal notice](https://newsroom.bmwgroup.com/global/legal-notice/)). No public press-image library was found for Perodua or Proton.

## Public datasets are pretraining material, apart from one Malaysian crawl

No curated, licensed benchmark for Malaysian make-and-model recognition was found. The established academic sets come from the US, China, the UK and the Czech Republic, and no make or model list was inspected for any of them, so "no Perodua or Proton" is an expectation rather than a verified fact.

| Dataset | Size | Origin | Terms | Role |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Stanford Cars | 16,185 images, 196 classes | US-market (unverified) | No licence stated on mirrors | Pretraining, sanity check |
| CompCars | 136,726 web images, 1,716 models | China | Non-commercial research, no redistribution | Pretraining |
| VMMRdb | 291,752 images, 9,170 classes | US classifieds | MIT on repo, image coverage unclear | Pretraining |
| DVM-CAR | 1,451,784 images, 899 models | UK | CC BY-NC 4.0 | Pretraining, background-removed |
| mesolitica dedup crawl | 97,598 images | Malaysian-market search terms | None stated | Main Malaysian pool after filtering |
| MY-VID v2 | 8,832 images, 47,975 boxes | Malaysian expressway | CC BY 4.0 | Detector fine-tuning or validation |

Stanford Cars needs care to obtain. Its original Stanford host has returned 404 since April 2023 and torchvision disabled automatic download in v0.18, so the data now lives on community mirrors on Kaggle, GitHub and Hugging Face ([pytorch/vision issue #7545](https://github.com/pytorch/vision/issues/7545)); the Hugging Face copy holds 8,144 training and 8,041 test rows ([HF tanganke/stanford_cars](https://huggingface.co/datasets/tanganke/stanford_cars)). CompCars adds bounding boxes and viewpoint labels, which suits a detect-then-classify pipeline, under non-commercial research terms that prohibit redistribution ([CompCars](https://mmlab.ie.cuhk.edu.hk/datasets/comp_cars/)). VMMRdb's user-shot photos from 712 areas are the closest in style to classifieds data ([VMMRdb](https://github.com/faezetta/VMMRdb)). DVM-CAR is the largest, but every image is 300x300 with the background removed, which differs from YOLO crops of street scenes ([DVM-CAR](https://deepvisualmarketing.github.io/)). Download links for these sets were not tested.

**The one substantial Malaysian resource is a pair of Hugging Face web-crawl dumps.** The raw version holds about 778,000 Google Images results keyed by 122 search keywords ([HF malaysia-ai crawl](https://huggingface.co/datasets/malaysia-ai/crawl-google-image-malaysian-vehicle)); the deduplicated version keeps 97,598 images in 13.5 GB, with keywords of the form "2021 Honda City 1.5 E", and includes motorcycles ([HF mesolitica dedup](https://huggingface.co/datasets/mesolitica/google-image-malaysian-vehicle-dedup)). Neither card states a licence, the keyword list was not enumerated, and per-model counts are unknown until the keyword field is tabulated. Labels are search terms, so expect interiors, brochures and wrong models. For the detection stage, MY-VID v2 from Sunway University supplies Malaysian expressway scenes in YOLO format under CC BY 4.0, though its six classes are vehicle types with no make or model ([Zenodo MY-VID](https://zenodo.org/records/17861464)).

Roboflow Universe has several small projects with Malaysian classes, such as a ten-class set including perodua-myvi, perodua-kancil, proton-saga and toyota-vios ([Roboflow car-model-by-joe](https://universe.roboflow.com/cloud-garden-playground/car-model-by-joe)) and a brand-level set with Perodua and Proton classes ([Roboflow Car Models](https://universe.roboflow.com/abc-pjocx/car-models-ves3u)), both CC BY 4.0. Image counts were not visible for any of them, and they need manual inspection before use. Malaysian-authored papers located either review international benchmarks, such as APU's own 2018 review ([JATI](https://jati.apu.edu.my/index.php/JATI/article/view/293)), or address vehicle type, plates or logos. That absence means there is no published Malaysian baseline to compare against, and results must be reported on the project's own held-out split. Kaggle, IEEE DataPort and local theses were searched thinly, so a relevant set could exist there undetected.

## Classifieds hold the most photos, but their terms close the door

Real-world sources rank very differently on volume and on permission, and the two orderings are nearly opposite.

**Classifieds are the largest labelled pool and the most restricted.** Mudah.my showed 4,161 live Myvi listings, 2,882 Vios, 2,761 City, 2,139 Saga and only 144 Mini Cooper on 15 November 2026 ([Mudah Myvi](https://www.mudah.my/malaysia/cars-for-sale/perodua/myvi), [Mudah Mini Cooper](https://www.mudah.my/malaysia/cars-for-sale/mini/cooper)), which also previews the class imbalance any Malaysian dataset will carry. Its terms prohibit spidering and scraping without prior written permission and bar copying or downloading site photographs without written consent ([Mudah Terms](https://www.mudah.my/about/index.htm?ca=9_s&page=terms)), and its robots.txt opens with a comment forbidding automated access without a special permit ([mudah.my/robots.txt](https://www.mudah.my/robots.txt)). Carlist.my reported 4,465 Myvi listings with year, make, model and variant encoded in listing URLs ([Carlist Myvi](https://www.carlist.my/cars-for-sale/perodua/myvi/malaysia)). The terms pages of Carlist, Carsome, myTukar, CARRO, Motor Trader and Caricarz all returned 403 or Cloudflare blocks and could not be read, so their positions are unverified; a permissive robots.txt on some of them is crawler etiquette, not a licence. The lawful routes are a written research request, manual saving of a small sample, or using only listing counts to plan class sizes.

**Automotive media are high quality and explicitly opt out.** WapCar's robots.txt carries an "ai-train=no" signal for all agents ([wapcar.my/robots.txt](https://www.wapcar.my/robots.txt)), Zigwheels disallows its picture-view paths ([zigwheels.my/robots.txt](https://www.zigwheels.my/robots.txt)), and paultan.org blocked automated requests outright. Treat these galleries as a visual reference for identifying facelifts, not as training data.

**Wikimedia Commons is the cleanest source and the smallest.** The Myvi category holds 243 files across three generation subcategories (44, 59 and 140), and recursive counts gave 576 for Proton Saga, 364 for Axia, 204 for X70, 1,002 for Honda City and just 28 for the X50 ([Commons API, Myvi](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&generator=categorymembers&gcmtitle=Category:Perodua_Myvi&gcmtype=subcat&prop=categoryinfo&format=json), [Commons search, Saga](https://commons.wikimedia.org/w/api.php?action=query&list=search&srsearch=deepcategory:%22Proton_Saga%22+filetype:bitmap&srnamespace=6&srinfo=totalhits&srlimit=1&format=json)). The generation subcategories supply generation labels almost for free, which is exactly what official sites lack. Wikimedia enforces per-minute API rate limits introduced in 2026, so downloads need a descriptive User-Agent, at most three concurrent requests and respect for Retry-After ([Wikimedia rate limits](https://www.mediawiki.org/wiki/Wikimedia_APIs/Rate_limits)). Flickr's search API filters by licence and returns up to 500 results per page ([Flickr API](https://www.flickr.com/services/api/flickr.photos.search.html)), and Openverse offers licence filters over a re-index that largely draws on Flickr and Commons, so its results must be deduplicated against both ([Openverse API](https://api.openverse.org/v1/schema/)). Keep a per-image licence and author manifest so attribution can be honoured.

**Search-engine collection has lost its official APIs.** Microsoft retired the Bing Search APIs on 11 August 2025 ([Microsoft Lifecycle](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/lifecycle/announcements/bing-search-api-retirement)), and Google's Custom Search JSON API is closed to new customers and ends on 1 January 2027 ([Google](https://developers.google.com/custom-search/v1/overview?hl=en)). Tutorials relying on either are obsolete for someone starting now. What remains are unofficial libraries that scrape result pages, such as icrawler and the actively released ddgs package ([icrawler](https://github.com/hellock/icrawler), [ddgs](https://pypi.org/project/ddgs/)); whether they currently work was not tested, and their output is the noisiest and least clear on copyright of any source here. The Hugging Face crawl already captures this kind of data, so re-scraping adds little.

**Self-collection is the only source the student fully owns.** It also matches deployment conditions best and is the natural home of the test set. The researchers estimate that one person can photograph several hundred distinct cars in an hour in a large car park, skewed heavily towards Myvi, Axia, Bezza, Saga, City and Vios; this is an estimate with no measured source, and rare classes such as MINI need targeted visits. YouTube walkaround frames are tempting but off limits: YouTube's terms prohibit downloading content without authorisation and bar automated access ([YouTube ToS](https://www.youtube.com/static?template=terms&gl=MY&hl=en)). On privacy, Malaysia's PDPA definition of personal data is tied to commercial transactions ([Baker McKenzie](https://resourcehub.bakermckenzie.com/en/resources/global-data-and-cyber-handbook/asia-pacific/malaysia/topics/key-definitions)), which suggests a non-commercial coursework project sits outside it, but no Malaysian guidance on number plates as personal data was found. Blurring plates and faces before sharing anything is the prudent default and also removes a shortcut feature. APU's own research-ethics requirements were not checked and should be.

The legal backdrop is unsettled rather than permissive. The Copyright Act's fair dealing exception expressly covers research and private study, judged on four factors including non-profit educational purpose and market effect ([WIPO Lex, Act A1420](https://www.wipo.int/wipolex/en/text/496717)). There is no text-and-data-mining exception yet; MyIPO opened a consultation dated 3 June 2026 proposing one, according to an industry response letter, and the outcome was not known ([ACT comment letter](https://actonline.org/wp-content/uploads/ACT-Comments-re-Malaysia-Copyright-Act-Consultation-13-Aug-2026.pdf)). Sources disagree on whether the current exception list is open or closed. Fair dealing is a case-by-case defence that does not override a website's contractual terms, so the low-risk posture is to keep copyrighted images private, publish only code, URL lists and weights, and prefer CC-licensed and self-shot images.

Synthetic images are a last-resort supplement. A Unity-rendering study found that synthetic data helped most with 256 or fewer real images and gave marginal benefit beyond 512, while synthetic-only models fell from 0.954 mean IoU on synthetic data to 0.618 on real images ([arXiv 2212.06232](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.06232)). That evidence is for detection and segmentation, not fine-grained classification, and no paper was found testing generated images for car model recognition.

## Forty clean images per class beats thousands of leaky ones

The benchmark evidence shifts the question from "how much data" to "how clean and how well split". Stanford Cars has roughly 41 training images per class ([TFDS cars196](https://www.tensorflow.org/datasets/catalog/cars196)), yet fine-tuned EfficientNet-B3 reaches 93.6% on it ([EfficientNet paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1905.11946)), and a linear head on frozen features reaches 88.2% with DINOv2 ViT-B/14 and 96.1% with OpenCLIP ViT-G/14 ([DINOv2 paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.07193)). By contrast, Ultralytics' YOLO26 classification models score 71.4% to 79.9% on ImageNet ([Ultralytics classify docs](https://docs.ultralytics.com/tasks/classify)). No source compares YOLO26-cls directly on a car benchmark, but the gap suggests keeping YOLO for detection and cropping and using a DINOv2, CLIP or EfficientNet-style backbone for the fine-grained head, with a frozen backbone plus linear probe as a cheap first baseline. A floor of 40 to 100 cleaned, varied real photos per class is a reasonable target extrapolated from these benchmarks, not a published rule.

**Define classes by what is visible, not by model year.** VMMRdb, which labels by make, model and year, managed only 51.76% top-1 against 92.90% top-5 with ResNet-50, and year confusion was its commonest error ([Tafazzoli et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017_workshops/w9/papers/Tafazzoli_A_Large_and_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf)). A two-step cascade on CompCars beat a flat classifier by only 1.4 points, 70.1% against 68.7% ([Sensors 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7830427)). The recommended design, which is reasoning rather than a cited result, is flat leaf classes named make_model_generation (for example perodua_myvi_gen3) with make and model kept as metadata, so the "Perodua → Perodua Myvi" hierarchy is reported by collapsing predictions rather than by training two classifiers. Split a facelift into its own class only when the exterior change is obvious and images are sufficient. Rebadged twins such as the Perodua Ativa and Toyota Raize differ mainly in badge and bumper, and no literature was found on separating them; expect side views to be ambiguous and report twin confusion separately. Use registration statistics to choose which models to include ([data.gov.my](https://data.gov.my/data-catalogue/registration_transactions_car)).

**Combine sources with provenance attached.** Every image should carry its source and a group key: listing ID, Commons file, photo session and vehicle, or manufacturer gallery. The evidence for mixing is direct: a model trained on CompCars dropped sharply on VMMRdb images of the same 51 classes, while training on the merged data was most robust on both ([Tafazzoli et al.](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_cvpr_2017_workshops/w9/papers/Tafazzoli_A_Large_and_CVPR_2017_paper.pdf)).

**Clean in a fixed order.** The suggested workflow is the researchers' synthesis of published practice. Drop broken files and exact duplicates by hash, which FiftyOne's recipe handles ([FiftyOne dedup](https://docs.voxel51.com/recipes/image_deduplication.html)). Run a COCO-pretrained YOLO detector over everything, keep the largest confident vehicle box, crop with a margin and discard images with no vehicle; this removes interiors, dashboards and brochures in one pass, the same class of junk VMMRdb's authors had to remove from classifieds data. Pad crops rather than centre-cropping, since Ultralytics warns that square crops can cut out important regions ([Ultralytics classify docs](https://docs.ultralytics.com/tasks/classify)). Remove near-duplicates by embedding similarity with fastdup, whose default threshold is 0.96 and whose licence is non-commercial ([fastdup](https://github.com/visual-layer/fastdup)). Once a first model exists, cleanlab's Datalab flags likely label errors from cross-validated predictions for hand review ([cleanlab](https://docs.cleanlab.ai/stable/tutorials/datalab/image.html)). Spot-check multi-car scenes, where the largest box is sometimes the wrong vehicle.

Do not trust zero-shot labelling for Malaysian models. CLIP ViT-B/32 scored only 0.21 on ten car models launched after 2023 ([arXiv 2510.18502](https://arxiv.org/html/2510.18502v1)), and no evaluation on Perodua or Proton was found. CLIP is safer as a junk filter and as an embedding source for a few-shot probe: about 4.8 labelled examples per class let a linear probe match zero-shot CLIP on Stanford Cars ([CLIP paper](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2103.00020)).

**Split by group, and test only on real photos.** A random image-level split on CompCars gave about 90% with ResNet-50; keeping near-identical images out of the test side gave about 61% ([Sensors 2021](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC7830427)). On Stanford Dogs, over 11% of test images were flagged as leaks, mostly burst shots of the same animal ([Voxel51](https://voxel51.com/blog/on-leaky-datasets-and-a-clever-horse)); several angles of one parked car, or 48 frames of one Proton spin, are the vehicle equivalent. So assign whole groups to one side with a group-aware splitter, then verify with FiftyOne's compute_leaky_splits, which the same article documents. Build the train, val and test folders by hand, because Ultralytics' automatic split is 80/20 at image level when no train folder exists ([Ultralytics datasets docs](https://docs.ultralytics.com/datasets/classify/)). Keep all renders, spin frames and synthetic images in training only, and draw the test set from self-shot photos taken at different locations from any training photos.

For tooling, YOLO26 is the latest released Ultralytics family, with YOLO27 still on a waitlist as of 10 November 2026 ([Ultralytics blog](https://www.ultralytics.com/blog/image-annotation-software-costs)); check the installed version on the day. Classification labels are folder membership, so a labelling platform is optional; Roboflow's free tier allows 20 projects on 10 credits a month if one is wanted for checking crops ([Roboflow pricing](https://roboflow.com/pricing)).

## Conclusion

The research inverts the intuitive plan. Manufacturer websites look like the authoritative source but contribute least to accuracy, while the sources that decide whether the model works on a real street (self-shot photos, Commons, a filtered crawl) are either small or noisy. The scarce resource is therefore not images but verified real-world examples of older generations, and effort is better spent photographing a car park and hand-checking a few hundred crops than writing a scraper for every brand site. A useful first experiment follows directly: train once on official renders alone and once with 12 to 24 real photos per class added, then score both on the self-shot test set. That measures the render-to-real gap that no published study covers for cars, and it turns the project's main data limitation into a finding.

The absence of any public Malaysian make-and-model benchmark cuts both ways. There is no baseline to beat, but a small, documented, group-split dataset with honest per-source accuracy would itself be a contribution, provided the images stay private and only code, URL manifests and weights are shared. Three things should be rechecked before relying on this report: the unread terms of Carlist, Carsome and the other blocked sites, the outcome of MyIPO's copyright consultation, and APU's ethics requirements for photographing vehicles in public.
